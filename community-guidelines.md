# Side C community guidelines

Last updated 7 November 2026 · Draft, to be checked by a solicitor before launch

Side C tapes are instructions, never clips: these guidelines keep it that way, and keep the hunt fun, fair and safe for everyone.

## The short version

Five rules cover almost everything; the sections below explain each one.

1. **Point, don't copy.** Tapes describe and link to pieces. They never contain the pieces themselves.
2. **Official sources only.** Every slot must be findable legally, without pirated copies.
3. **Leave private people out.** Only use clips of people who chose to be public.
4. **Unsettling is fine; harmful isn't.** Strange and eerie, yes. Graphic, cruel or dangerous, no.
5. **Be honest and kind.** Label tapes accurately, hide spoilers, and treat other collectors well.

## Sources

Every slot must point to something a collector can watch or hear legally: an official channel, a licensed service, a broadcaster's own archive, a library, or a copy they own.

- **Link only to official sources.** An artist's or broadcaster's own channel, a streaming or catch-up service, a public archive. If you're not sure a link is official, leave the link out and describe where to look instead.
- **No pirated copies or unofficial re-uploads,** even if that's the easiest place to find something.
- **No download instructions.** Don't tell collectors how to rip, record or save a clip.
- **Say what it costs.** Mark each slot as free, subscription, rent or buy, archive, or do it yourself.
- **Give a fallback for anything hard to reach.** Clips disappear and services change. A fallback keeps your tape finishable.

## Describe, don't copy

A tape is your description of a moment, in your own words, plus where and when to find it.

- **No transcripts, scripts or lyrics.** Don't paste what's said or sung. Describe it instead: "the moment the presenter gives up and laughs" rather than the line itself.
- **No uploads of the pieces.** No audio, video, screenshots or stills from the sources. Your own J-card art and notes are welcome.
- **Keep slots to moments.** Clips run 5 seconds to 2 minutes, and most work best under one. In-between slots run 3 seconds to 1 minute.
- **Never a whole episode, scene or song.** If a slot would cover most of something, cut it down or choose a different moment.

| Slot type | Shortest | Longest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clip | 5 seconds | 2 minutes |
| In-between | 3 seconds | 1 minute |
| Interference | 10 seconds | 45 seconds |

## People and privacy

Only use clips of people who chose to be seen or heard in public: performers, presenters, public figures, people speaking on a broadcast.

- **No private individuals.** No clips of people filmed without knowing, private videos, or people who've gone viral by accident and asked to be left alone.
- **Never ask collectors to record other people.** In-between slots can't involve filming, recording or approaching anyone.
- **No personal details.** No names, addresses, workplaces or other details about private people, in tapes, notes, hints or comments.
- **Don't mock people for who they are.** Laughing at a moment is fine. Targeting someone's appearance, disability, background or identity isn't.

## Interference and unsettling content

Strange, eerie and uncomfortable are part of Side C; the unease should come from how pieces are paired, not from upsetting material.

- **Unsettling, not graphic.** No real violence, injury, death, cruelty to people or animals, or sexual content, in any slot.
- **No real tragedies played for effect.** Don't use news footage of disasters, attacks or real victims to create unease.
- **Keep Interference short and rare.** 10 to 45 seconds, at most two per tape, with a clear sync cue.
- **Warn people.** Flag flashing images, sudden loud noises, upsetting themes and any Interference slot. Collectors see these before they start.
- **No hate.** Nothing that attacks people for their race, religion, gender, sexuality, disability or nationality, including as a "clash".

## In-between instructions

In-between slots must be safe, legal and free for anyone to do in an ordinary room.

- **Good:** turn the lights off, sit in silence, read a line aloud, open a window, tap a rhythm on the table, make a cup of tea.
- **Not allowed:** anything involving heat, flames, sharp objects, heights or water hazards; anything that needs leaving the house or going somewhere at night; eating, drinking or taking anything unusual; holding your breath, spinning or anything physically risky.
- **Nothing that costs money** or needs special equipment.
- **Nothing that involves other people** without them choosing to join in.
- **Keep it short:** 3 seconds to 1 minute.

## Honest labels

Collectors choose tapes by their labels, so every label should match how the tape really plays.

- **Vibes and vibe arc:** pick the ones that describe how it feels, not the ones you think will get the most plays.
- **Regions:** mark where each slot is available. Only call a tape Worldwide if every slot, or one of its fallbacks, works anywhere.
- **Tier and difficulty:** keep the running time within 10% of the tier, and rate difficulty fairly. Archive digging is not "quick".
- **Spoilers:** if a slot gives away a plot twist or ending, flag it on that slot.
- **Mystery and locked slots:** clues must be solvable, and locked slots must unlock from a slot that's actually findable.

## Hints, responses and remixes

The community side works when people help each other find things without spoiling the hunt.

- **Hints nudge, they don't solve.** "Try children's TV from the early 90s" is a hint. A direct link to the answer is a spoiler.
- **Hide spoilers in "I made this" responses.** Use the spoiler option for anything about the reveal or a mystery slot.
- **Be kind about other people's tapes.** Say what worked and what didn't, without insulting the creator.
- **Remixes credit the original.** A remix links back to the tape it came from, and the original creator's notes stay theirs.
- **No spam or self-promotion** in hints, responses or comments.

## Review, reporting and what happens when rules are broken

Every tape is reviewed before it goes live, and anyone can report a problem afterwards.

**How review works**

1. You submit a tape. It stays a draft only you can see.
2. A reviewer checks it against these guidelines: sources, descriptions, safety, labels.
3. It's published, or sent back with notes on what to change. You can edit and resubmit as often as you like.

**Reporting**

- **Dead link or missing source:** use "This slot is broken" on the slot. The creator is asked to update it, and collectors are pointed to the fallbacks meanwhile.
- **A rule being broken:** use "Report" on the tape, hint or response, and say which rule. Reports are private.

**What happens next**

| Situation | What we do |
| --- | --- |
| A small, fixable problem (a mislabelled vibe, a missing warning) | Ask the creator to fix it; the tape stays up |
| A broken rule in one slot (a pirated link, a transcript) | Hide the tape until it's fixed |
| A serious or repeated breach | Remove the tape and pause the account |
| Harassment, hate, exposing private people, or anything dangerous | Remove it straight away and close the account |

If you think we got it wrong, you can ask for a second review.

## Rights holders and age

Side C hosts no clips, but rights holders can still ask for a tape or slot to come down, and accounts have a minimum age.

- **Takedown requests:** a rights holder can use the [CONTACT] form to ask for a slot or tape to be removed. We'll respond within [TIME] and tell the creator what was removed and why.
- **Minimum age:** you must be [AGE] or older to make an account.
